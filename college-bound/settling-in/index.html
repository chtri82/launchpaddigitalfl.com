College-bound in Florida · Part 4 · Settling in

Settling into college life

By the Launchpad Digital staff · Edited by Charlie Triantafilou, Editor, Launchpad Digital · Last checked October 2026.

Informational, not legal or financial advice. Rules change and every school sets its own policies; confirm with your school and the agencies linked here. We are nonpartisan.

Part of our series: College-bound in Florida.

This part is practical advice rather than statistics. Where we point to a rule, we link to it.

Build a weekly schedule

College classes meet fewer hours than high school, and much of the work happens on your own time. Put classes, study blocks, work shifts and sleep on one calendar during the first week. Find each syllabus's exam dates and add them right away.

Protect your scholarship

Bright Futures has a GPA to keep (3.0 for FAS, 2.75 for FMS) and credit-hour rules each term (Florida DOE). Before you drop a class, check with financial aid how it affects your hours.

Money basics

Write down a monthly budget for food beyond your meal plan, transportation, books and phone. Know when aid refunds arrive and what they need to cover. Read any loan terms on studentaid.gov before you accept.

Roommates

Most housing offices use roommate agreements. Settle sleep times, guests, cleaning and shared items early. Your RA can help mediate.

Get involved, a little

Join one or two student organizations, intramurals or a campus job. That's a common way to find your people without overloading your schedule.

Safety

Save your campus police number and sign up for your school's emergency alerts. Florida students should also know their hall's hurricane procedures.

Homesickness is normal

If it lasts or affects classes, talk to the counseling center (see Part 3).
