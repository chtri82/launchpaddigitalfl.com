ECB President Christine Lagarde Will Step Down Feb. 7, 2027

The European Central Bank says Lagarde told its Governing Council and European Council President António Costa on Thursday. The European Council will appoint her successor.

By Nathan Cole · Published Oct. 29, 2026

AI-assisted journalist

Updated Oct 30, 2026, 1:35 PM ET: added the ECB's response to Launchpad Digital's questions, the statute rule on who chairs the bank without a president, and how her successor is chosen.

Updated Oct 29, 2026, 4:50 PM ET: added Lagarde's explanation from Thursday's ECB press conference and the bank's interest rate decision.

FRANKFURT, Germany, Oct. 29, 2026 —

European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde will step down on Feb. 7, 2027, the bank said Thursday in a press release.

The ECB says Lagarde informed the bank's Governing Council and the President of the European Council, António Costa, of her decision on Thursday. The bank announced it on X at 6:00 a.m. Eastern time.

"I have just informed my colleagues that I will step down as @ECB President on 7 February 2027," Lagarde wrote on X minutes later. "It has been a source of great pride to serve this institution. I thank all of those who have supported me on this journey over the past seven years."

What happens next

  • Her successor. The ECB says the next president will be appointed by the European Council following the nomination process in Article 283 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (ECB). The Council of the EU describes that process on its appointments page.
  • The Governing Council's reaction. The ECB says the Governing Council "thanked President Lagarde warmly and gave her a long standing ovation."
  • Today's rate decision. The ECB said on X that Lagarde will explain the bank's latest monetary policy decisions at a live press conference later Thursday (ECB on X).

The ECB sets interest rates for the countries that use the euro (ECB monetary policy). The morning announcement did not give a reason for her decision.

Why February, in her words

At the ECB's press conference in Frankfurt later Thursday, Lagarde said her term "would have been" up in October 2027 in any event (ECB transcript).

"The reason I'm anticipating the end of my term, by leaving in February of 2027 instead of October is, as I have said to colleagues, members of staff and governors, because I want to have the freedom of speech, the freedom of expression and the freedom to engage on matters that are not monetary policy matters," she said.

She said she'll step down after the February monetary policy meeting, and that "until 7 February I'm in charge." On who comes next, she said "A process has already started to identify my successor."

The ECB also left its three key interest rates unchanged on Thursday, with the deposit rate at 2.50%. Lagarde said the Governing Council "debated whether a hike was appropriate" before deciding unanimously to hold.

What the ECB told Launchpad Digital

Eszter Miltényi-Torstensson, deputy head of newsroom at the European Central Bank, answered Launchpad Digital's questions by email on Friday.

"Article 13 and Article 45 of the Statute of the ESCB and the ECB state that, in the absence of the President, the Vice-President shall chair the Governing Council and the Executive Board of the ECB," she wrote.

Article 13.1 of the statute reads: "The President or, in his absence, the Vice-President shall chair the Governing Council and the Executive Board of the ECB." Article 45.1 says the same for the bank's General Council (Statute of the ESCB and of the ECB, EUR-Lex). The ECB's vice-president is Boris Vujčić, who took office June 1, 2026 (Council of the EU).

On when a successor will be named, she wrote: "I cannot estimate the timeline." She said it was announced Friday "that there is a European Council meeting on 26-27 November to discuss it." Launchpad Digital could not yet find that meeting on an official European Council page.

How the next president is chosen. According to the Council of the EU, the European Council appoints the ECB president on a recommendation from the Council, after consulting the European Parliament and the ECB Governing Council. Leaders of the euro area countries decide by reinforced qualified majority, which means at least 16 of the 21 euro countries, representing at least 65% of the euro area population. The president serves one eight-year term that can't be renewed and must be a national of a euro area country. European Council President António Costa said Thursday the process "will be initiated today and will proceed without delay" (European Council).

Miltényi-Torstensson also pointed to what Lagarde said at Thursday's press conference: "I will be replaced, so rest assured that not only the ECB is going to carry on with perfect continuity of business. A process has already started to identify my successor. There are plenty of excellent candidates, much better than me, actually. So don't worry about continuity of business. Don't worry about decision making in limbo. No, absolutely not. The institution is solid, strong, like a rock, and it will continue being so" (ECB transcript).

Those are the facts, directly from the source.
