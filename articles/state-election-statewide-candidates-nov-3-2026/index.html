Who's Running Statewide in Florida on Nov. 3: The Qualified Candidates, From the State's Own List

The Division of Elections lists qualified candidates for U.S. Senate, governor, attorney general, chief financial officer and agriculture commissioner. Here they are, in the order the state lists them.

By Grant Holloway · Published Oct. 27, 2026

AI-assisted journalist

TALLAHASSEE, Oct. 27, 2026 —

With early voting underway and Election Day one week out, the Florida Division of Elections' candidate listing for the 2026 General Election shows who has qualified for the statewide offices on the Nov. 3 ballot. Launchpad checked the list Tuesday night.

Party labels below are the ones the state uses: REP (Republican), DEM (Democrat), NPA (no party affiliation), LPF (Libertarian Party of Florida) and WRI (write-in). Candidates are listed alphabetically, as the state lists them, and "incumbent" is the state's own notation.

U.S. Senate

  • Neil J. Gillespie (NPA)
  • Ashley Moody (REP), incumbent
  • Angie Nixon (DEM)

Governor and lieutenant governor

The state lists 10 qualified tickets:

  • Dean Ocean Abrams and Joe Van Vactor (NPA)
  • Kathy Anderson and Daniel Nokovich (WRI)
  • Charles Burkett and Ruben A. Coto (NPA)
  • Jeffrey Peter "Dr. Jeff" Datto and Juan Santana (NPA)
  • Moliere "Moe" Dimanche and Benjiman Rojas (NPA)
  • Byron Donalds and Bryan Avila (REP)
  • Scott Eckhard Jewett and Nicole Skelly (LPF)
  • David Jolly and Gwen Graham (DEM)
  • Erik Morris and LeRoy Alaways (WRI)
  • Frank J. Russo and Rachel Rodriguez (NPA)

Attorney general

  • Jose Javier Rodriguez (DEM)
  • James Uthmeier (REP), incumbent

Chief financial officer

  • Blaise Ingoglia (REP), incumbent
  • Annette Taddeo (DEM)

Commissioner of agriculture

  • Kyle "KC" Gibson (WRI)
  • Joey Mendoza Atkins (DEM)
  • Wilton Simpson (REP), incumbent

Also on the state's list

The same listing shows qualified candidates in all 28 of Florida's U.S. House districts, state Senate and state House districts, and judicial races. It lists Florida Supreme Court Justice Carlos G. Muniz and 22 district court of appeal judges as qualified. Your own ballot depends on where you live.

Where to check your own ballot

The Division of Elections describes its candidate tracking system as an "unofficial reference" and says that for county or municipal candidates, voters should contact their local Supervisor of Elections, according to its Candidate Tracking System page. Contact information for every county office is on the state's Supervisor of Elections directory.

For early voting dates, the mail ballot deadline and ID rules, see our story on Florida's Nov. 3 voting dates.

Launchpad Digital does not endorse candidates or parties. Those are the names on the state's list; the choice is yours.

Source: Florida Division of Elections candidate listing and Candidate Tracking System page, checked Oct. 27, 2026.
