Florida Files Racketeering Charges in Cocaine Pipeline Investigators Tie to Sinaloa Cartel

Attorney General James Uthmeier and Pinellas County Sheriff Bob Gualtieri announced the new charges Tuesday in "Operation Hall of Fame," a case that began with a 60-kilogram cocaine seizure in July.

By Maya Reyes · Published Oct. 27, 2026

AI-assisted journalist

TAMPA, Fla., Oct. 27, 2026 —

Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier and Pinellas County Sheriff Bob Gualtieri announced racketeering and conspiracy to commit racketeering charges Tuesday against people accused of running a cocaine pipeline from Texas to Tampa Bay, FOX 13 Tampa Bay reported. Officials said the network was linked to Mexico's Sinaloa Cartel. WFLA also covered the Tampa news conference.

What officials say

  • Uthmeier said investigators have made 10 arrests, including a person officials described as a major cartel leader, The Epoch Times reported. He said more arrests could be announced soon.
  • The new racketeering counts are in addition to the trafficking charges filed over the summer, according to FOX 13.
  • Officials said about $2.1 million worth of cocaine was seized.

How the case started

  • The Pinellas County Sheriff's Office says detectives watched a shipment on July 10, 2026, which led to nine arrests and the seizure of 60 kilograms of cocaine. The sheriff's office says the cocaine was being moved by truck tractor from El Paso, Texas, to Pinellas County.
  • Investigators with the sheriff's office and the Florida Department of Law Enforcement used wiretaps during a three-year investigation, Gualtieri said, according to FOX 13. Officials said the group used two truck tractors that did not run as props, so it looked like the vehicles were being towed.
  • Daniel Pinales, 35, of Brandon, whom authorities identified as the head of the ring, remains in jail on $10.1 million bail, FOX 13 reported.

Charges are accusations. Everyone charged is presumed innocent unless proven guilty in court. This is a developing story, and we will update it as the Attorney General's Office and the sheriff's office release more details.

Those are the facts, directly from the source.

Sources: Pinellas County Sheriff's Office (July 2026 release); FOX 13 Tampa Bay, WFLA and The Epoch Times reporting on the Oct. 27, 2026 news conference.
