Florida Charges 10 in "Operation Hall of Fame" Racketeering Case Tied to Sinaloa Cartel Cocaine Pipeline

The Attorney General's Office says the Office of Statewide Prosecution has filed racketeering and conspiracy charges against defendants it links to a Sinaloa Cartel enterprise that moved cocaine from Texas to the Tampa Bay area.

By Maya Reyes · Published Oct. 27, 2026

AI-assisted journalist

TAMPA, FL, Oct. 27, 2026 —

Updated Oct. 27, 2026, 2:51 p.m. ET: Adds video of the Tampa news conference and officials' remarks checked against that recording.

Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier and Pinellas County Sheriff Bob Gualtieri announced Tuesday that prosecutors have charged multiple people with racketeering and conspiracy to commit racketeering in a case the state calls "Operation Hall of Fame." In a news release, the Attorney General's Office says the defendants were part of a Sinaloa Cartel-linked enterprise that moved thousands of kilograms of cocaine from Mexico, through El Paso, Texas, into the Tampa Bay area. The headline on the release says a cartel boss and nine others were arrested.

Charges are accusations. Everyone charged is presumed innocent unless proven guilty in court.

What the Attorney General's Office says

  • The charges. Ten defendants are charged with Conspiracy to Commit Racketeering, a first-degree felony under Florida Statute 895.03(4): Daniel Pinales, Erick Manuel Gomez Tapia, Jesus Morales, Joaquin Enriquez, Arturo Carlos, Jorge Ramiro Zamonsett, Tyler Green, Marice Higgins, Miguel Alejandro Carrasco-Lechuga and Brian Varner. Four of them (Pinales, Gomez Tapia, Morales and Enriquez) also face a racketeering charge, a first-degree felony under Florida Statute 895.03(3).
  • A possible life sentence. The release says Pinales also faces a life sentence in connection with Conspiracy to Traffic in Cocaine over 150 kilograms.
  • How the enterprise worked. The release says it operated as a Sinaloa Cartel branch based in Juarez, Mexico. Cocaine was delivered to Zamonsett in the El Paso area, who arranged commercial semi-trucks and chase vehicles to take it to Pinales in the Tampa Bay region. Pinales then distributed it through a network that included Green, Higgins and Varner. Investigators documented a monthly pattern of deliveries from at least December 2025 through July 2026, the release says.
  • The July seizure. On July 11, 2026, Pinellas County Sheriff's Office detectives working with the Florida Department of Law Enforcement (FDLE) saw three duffle bags holding about 60 kilograms of cocaine handed to Pinales, the release says. The cocaine field-tested positive and Pinales was arrested. The state filed the first charges in Pasco County.
  • Later arrests and bonds. The release says Gomez Tapia, described as the organization's number two and also known as "Canelo," was arrested on a Florida warrant with a $30 million bond and is in custody in Texas awaiting extradition. Zamonsett is charged as a co-conspirator and has a $10 million bond. Carrasco-Lechuga was arrested and is held at the Pasco County Detention Center on a $1 million bond. The release puts the bonds on the original defendants at about $40 million in total.
  • More seizures. Information from the investigation helped partner agencies seize 99 kilograms of cocaine in the El Paso area on July 29, 2026, and 60 pounds of methamphetamine on Oct. 20, 2026, according to the release.
  • Who worked the case. The release credits the Pinellas County Sheriff's Office Narcotics Unit, which it says formed a task force that worked in Texas for weeks, along with FDLE, Homeland Security Investigations and the El Paso Police Department. Office of Statewide Prosecution Special Counsel Ryan Shaw and Special Assistant Statewide Prosecutor Bryan Sarabia of the Sixth Judicial Circuit are prosecuting the cases.

What the officials said

"This investigation has completely dismantled a major Sinaloa Cartel pipeline that was flooding Florida communities with thousands of kilograms of cocaine," Uthmeier said in the release.

"This is the largest drug seizure from a single Pinellas County drug trafficker in the past 40 years," Gualtieri said in the release. "The amount of cocaine that Daniel Pinales was putting on the streets of Pinellas County is simply astronomical."

Background from the sheriff's office

The Pinellas County Sheriff's Office described the case in a July release (26-066). It says that after several years of investigating a local drug trafficker, detectives learned cocaine was being moved by truck tractor from El Paso to Pinellas County. It says detectives watched one of those transports on July 10, 2026, which led to nine arrests and the seizure of 60 kilograms of cocaine. Sheriff Gualtieri and FDLE Special Agent in Charge Mark Dubina held a news conference on the case on July 14. The attorney general's release dates the seizure to July 11, and at the news conference both Shaw (4:25) and Gualtieri (10:27) gave July 11 as the date.

Watch the news conference

What officials said at the news conference

Uthmeier, Shaw and Gualtieri spoke for about 18 minutes and took one question on the case.

  • Ten arrests, more possible. "So today we're here to announce that we've made 10 arrests, including one of the cartel's top bosses," Uthmeier said (1:36). He added that the new charges "go along with some of the initial trafficking charges that were filed back in the summer and leads may continue and more arrests may be announced soon" (1:55).
  • How many are charged. Shaw, special counsel in the Office of Statewide Prosecution, said: "Ten individuals charged here in Dade City, Pasco County, two in Pinellas County, 12 people total, 10 charged with conspiracy to RICO. Several of those now charged with RICO" (8:55). The written release lists 10 defendants; the two Pinellas County cases aren't named in it.
  • How much moved. "From just December of '25 to July of '26, we're talking thousands of kilos were flowing into this area. All of that is now stopped," Shaw said (6:27).
  • Pinellas streets. Gualtieri said: "I believe the evidence is clearly going to establish that this drug trafficking organization is responsible for putting well over 500 kilograms of cocaine on the streets of Pinellas County" (12:01).
  • Pinales' bail. "Daniel Pinales remains in jail today on a $10.1 million bail, which is astronomically high, as it should be for somebody who is a danger to the community and somebody who is absolutely a flight risk," Gualtieri said (10:38).
  • Possible sentence. Gualtieri said Pinales "is facing a minimum mandatory of life in state prison" (14:13).
  • Stash houses and forfeited trucks. Gualtieri said cocaine delivered to Pinales was taken to stash houses in Hillsborough County and sold to street-level distributors (12:48). He said the courts "have already ordered the forfeiture" of the tractors used to hide the cocaine to the Pinellas County Sheriff's Office (12:35).
  • Value of the July seizure. Gualtieri put the 60 kilograms seized in July at about $2.1 million, which he described as the wholesale value (13:24).
  • Beyond Florida. "This organization was distributing cocaine not just here in Florida, but in Illinois and Pennsylvania and all these Midwest and Northeast locations as well," Gualtieri said (15:54).
  • When the investigation began. Asked how long the ring had operated in Tampa Bay, Uthmeier said, "It appears it's been in this area for some time," and that "observations began in late 2025" (18:09). Gualtieri said earlier that law enforcement had received information about Pinales "for a really, really long time" (9:45).

The wiretap and three-year-investigation details that some outlets attributed to the news conference weren't said in this recording, so they are not in this story.
