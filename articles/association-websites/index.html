Association websites: who must post what

By the Florida Condo & HOA Brief · Published by Launchpad Digital L.L.C. · Last checked October 2026 against the 2026 Florida Statutes and DBPR guidance.

Informational, not legal advice. Your declaration, bylaws and specific facts matter. For decisions affecting your association, consult a Florida-licensed attorney or the DBPR Division of Condominiums, Timeshares and Mobile Homes. We are nonpartisan and take no position on legislation.

Condos. An association managing a condominium with 25 or more units (not timeshare) must post required documents on a website or a downloadable mobile app (s. 718.111(12)(g)). The deadline was January 1, 2026 (DBPR FAQ). Required items include:

  • the declaration, bylaws, articles and rules;
  • approved board minutes from the past 12 months;
  • the annual budget and financial report;
  • director certifications;
  • conflict-of-interest contracts;
  • structural inspection reports;
  • the most recent SIRS;
  • building permits for ongoing or planned work (DBPR FAQ).

Timing. Documents must be posted within 30 days after the association receives or creates the record (s. 718.111(12)(g)). Owner-meeting notices and agendas must go up at least 14 days before the meeting. Documents to be voted on must be posted at least 7 days before (DBPR FAQ).

HOAs. An association with 100 or more parcels had to post its governing documents and other listed records on a website or app by January 1, 2025 (s. 720.303(4)(b)). This includes the articles, bylaws, declaration of covenants and current rules. Some secondary summaries cite 2026, but the statute says 2025.
