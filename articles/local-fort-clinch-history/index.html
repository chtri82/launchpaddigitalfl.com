Cannons, Shark Teeth and 300 Years of History at Fernandina Beach's Fort Clinch

A Florida state park on Amelia Island holds one of the best-preserved 19th-century forts in the country. Here's its history and what visitors can do there.

By Eden, Launchpad Digital · Published Oct. 25, 2026

FERNANDINA BEACH, Fla., Oct. 25, 2026 — At the northern tip of Florida's Atlantic coast, a row of cannons points across the St. Marys River toward Georgia. Florida State Parks calls them "silent testimony to the strategic importance of Fort Clinch during the Civil War" (Florida State Parks).

Nearly 300 years on guard

The entrance to Cumberland Sound and the St. Marys River has mattered to Floridians for close to 300 years, according to the state park's history page. The first fortifications on the site began in 1736 (Florida State Parks).

Construction of Fort Clinch as it stands today began in 1847 (Florida State Parks). When the Civil War broke out, only about two-thirds of the fort was finished and no cannons had been mounted, the state says (Florida State Parks). The fort first came under Confederate control. Union troops arrived in March 1862 after Gen. Robert E. Lee ordered an evacuation (Florida State Parks).

The fort was used again as a barracks and ammunition depot during the Spanish-American War in 1898, according to the park (Florida State Parks). The Army sold it to private owners in 1926, and it became one of Florida's first state parks in 1935 (Florida State Parks). Civilian Conservation Corps Company 1420 began restoring it in 1936 and built the museum, campground and park roads (Florida State Parks). During World War II, the fort served as a joint operations center for surveillance and communications (Florida State Parks).

"Although it was never fully completed or used in direct combat, Fort Clinch served as a military post during three U.S. engagements," the state says (Florida State Parks).

Step inside

Visitors can tour bastions, guard rooms, a prison, barracks, a hospital, kitchens and a blacksmith's shop, furnished as they would have been during the Civil War, according to the park (Florida State Parks). Staff and volunteers in period uniforms show daily life, from bullet-making to sewing (Florida State Parks).

On the first weekend of every month, the park says, a soldier garrison fires cannons and demonstrates other battlefield skills (Florida State Parks). Other events include candlelight tours and larger reenactments (Florida State Parks).

Beyond the walls

The fort is just one part of the 1,400-acre park, which has maritime hammocks with "massive arching live oaks" along its hiking and biking trails (Florida State Parks). The park says it is known for gopher tortoises and painted buntings. Camping, fishing, shelling and shark-tooth hunting are popular (Florida State Parks). According to the park, sharks have patrolled Florida's waters "since the last Ice Age, ensuring an endless supply of shark teeth" on its beaches (Florida State Parks).

Plan your visit

  • Address: 2601 Atlantic Ave., Fernandina Beach (Florida State Parks)
  • Park hours: 8 a.m. to sunset, 365 days a year. The fort is open 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., and the visitor center 9 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. (Florida State Parks)
  • Fee: $6 per vehicle (Florida State Parks)
  • Phone: 904-277-7274 (Florida State Parks)
