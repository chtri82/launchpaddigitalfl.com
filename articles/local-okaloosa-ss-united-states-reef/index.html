The Fastest Ocean Liner Ever Built Is Set to Become the World's Largest Artificial Reef

Okaloosa County says the SS United States will go down in the Gulf about 22 nautical miles off Destin-Fort Walton Beach during the second week of November, weather permitting.

By Grant Holloway · Published Oct. 25, 2026

AI-assisted journalist

FORT WALTON BEACH, Fla. — Oct. 25, 2026 — A famous American ship is close to its final voyage. Okaloosa County says the SS United States will be deployed as an artificial reef in the Gulf, about 22 nautical miles southwest of Destin-Fort Walton Beach, during the second week of November 2026.[1]

The county stresses that the plan is "weather-dependent and dates may change." It says more details "will be released in the coming weeks."[1]

A record-setting ship

According to the county, the SS United States was launched in 1951 and "still holds the world record for the fastest oceanliner."[2] The county says the ship will now "break another record as the World's Largest Artificial Reef."[2]

The ship was retired from service in 1969, which the county attributes to "the rise and popularity of commercial air travel."[2] Over the years it had multiple owners, nearly all items were removed from its decks, and its engines were made unable to run.[2]

How it ended up in Florida

The county says the ship faced being sold for scrap after nearly 30 years at the same location in Philadelphia.[2] "With all other options exhausted," Okaloosa County agreed to buy the vessel for the sole purpose of making it an artificial reef to boost Gulf marine habitat and tourism.[2]

Why not restore it? The county says efforts by multiple groups over more than two decades couldn't overcome the cost and the challenge of finding a place to dock it for public visits. It calls its own reef spending "merely a fraction of the hundreds of millions of dollars" a full restoration would cost.[2]

Getting ready to sink

The ship was prepared in Mobile, Alabama.[2] Before deployment, the county says, all environmentally unsafe materials are removed, including all non-metal items, wiring and fuel.[2]

The county says it follows federal standards, including the Environmental Protection Agency and Maritime Administration's Best Management Practices for Preparing Vessels Intended to Create Artificial Reefs, and an Army Corps of Engineers permit.[2]

Some people have raised concerns about a coating on the ship. The county says that substance "is not a federally regulated substance when it comes to artificial reefing."[2] Readers who want more detail can review the county's FAQ and the December 2025 project presentation it links.[2]

Why a reef?

Artificial reefs, the county says, "create habitat that did not previously exist." It says they benefit marine life, the fishing industry and tourism.[2]

The county called the deployment "an exciting move forward to create a one-of-a-kind resource for the environment and for our tourism industry."[1]

Want to know more?

The county's October newsletter also lists the deployment for the second week of November.[3] Questions can go to Okaloosa County Public Information Officer Nick Tomecek at 850-651-7321.[1]
