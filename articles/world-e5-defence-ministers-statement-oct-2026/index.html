Five European Defense Ministers Pledge a Bigger Role for Europe Within NATO

The defense ministers of France, Germany, Italy, Poland and the United Kingdom said the U.S. "is and will remain an indispensable Ally" and reaffirmed support for Ukraine.

By Grant Holloway · Published Oct. 28, 2026

AI-assisted journalist

LONDON, Oct. 28, 2026 —

The defense ministers of France, Germany, Italy, Poland and the United Kingdom, a group that calls itself the E5, issued a joint statement saying "Europe must assume greater responsibility for its own security," according to the statement published by the U.K. government on Oct. 26.

What the ministers said

  • On Russia. The five countries "share the assessment that Russia represents the most acute and long-term threat to Euro-Atlantic security and stability," citing the war against Ukraine, violations of allied airspace, sabotage and what they call "multi-domain hybrid attacks."
  • On the United States. "The United States is and will remain an indispensable Ally, and continued United States engagement in European security is essential to a strong and credible NATO," the statement says.
  • On NATO. The ministers call NATO "the cornerstone of our collective security" and say they will "assume greater political and military leadership within the Alliance," including closer cooperation among their defense industries.
  • On the European Union. They say EU defense efforts "should complement NATO, avoid duplication" and remain open, under certain conditions, to cooperation with NATO allies outside the EU.
  • On Ukraine. The ministers say they will keep supporting Ukraine "politically, militarily and financially," continue acting against Russia's "shadow fleet" of ships in line with international law, and that a coalition of countries will contribute security guarantees "once a cease-fire or a peace agreement is in place."

The statement doesn't announce specific new spending figures or troop deployments.

Those are the facts, directly from the source.

Source: E5 Defence Ministers' Statement, Oct. 26, 2026, published by the U.K. Ministry of Defence on GOV.UK.
