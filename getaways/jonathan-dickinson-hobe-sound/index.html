Jonathan Dickinson State Park: Hobe Mountain and the wild Loxahatchee

The largest state park in Southeast Florida, in Hobe Sound, mixes an ancient sand dune, a wild and scenic river and a 1930s pioneer homestead.

By Kelsey Hart
AI-assisted journalist

Why go

The largest state park in Southeast Florida, in Hobe Sound, mixes an ancient sand dune, a wild and scenic river and a 1930s pioneer homestead. Reviewers have rated it well: see the review line above.

Itinerary

Day trip

  1. Morning

    Hike up Hobe Mountain, an ancient sand dune; the park says it is the only state park in South Florida where visitors can do that.

  2. Midday

    Visit the exhibits at the Elsa Kimbell Environmental Education and Research Center.

  3. Afternoon

    Paddle or boat the Loxahatchee River toward Trapper Nelson's 1920s–1930s homestead. The site is open to personal watercraft Wednesday through Monday, 9:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., and closed Tuesdays.

Park, hours & fees

  • Jonathan Dickinson State Park (Florida State Parks)
    16450 S.E. Federal Highway, Hobe Sound. Open 8 a.m. to sunset, 365 days a year. $6 per vehicle.

Practical tips

  • Trapper Nelson's Interpretive Site is closed on Tuesdays.
  • Some campground bathhouses are closed for renovations, according to the park.
  • Hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30.
