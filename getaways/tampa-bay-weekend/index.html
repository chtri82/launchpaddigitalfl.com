Two days on Tampa Bay: Ybor City and the Gulf beaches

Historic Ybor City on day one, a Gulf barrier island on day two.

As of October 2026, check hours, fees and closures before you go.

At a glance

From Palm Bay
About 2½ hours west (estimate, no traffic)
Length
2 days
Region
Tampa Bay

Itinerary

Day 1

  1. Morning

    Drive west to Tampa and start in Ybor City, the historic cigar-making district. Ybor City Museum State Park covers its history.

  2. Afternoon

    Lunch at the Columbia Restaurant on 7th Avenue, then walk the district.

  3. Evening

    Dinner at Bern's Steak House in South Tampa. Reservations are recommended.

Day 2

  1. Morning

    Head to Honeymoon Island State Park in Dunedin for the beach and trails. The ferry to Caladesi Island leaves from the park.

  2. Afternoon

    Smoked fish at Ted Peters in the St. Petersburg area, then the drive home, about 2½ to 3 hours.

Parks & fees

  • Honeymoon Island State Park (Florida State Parks)
    Open 8 a.m. to sunset. $8 per vehicle. Ferry terminal for Caladesi Island. Use caution near the cut at the north sandspit.
  • Ybor City Museum State Park (Official site)
    Check the official site for fees and hours; we could not verify them for this guide.

Fees and hours as listed on each official page when we checked in October 2026.

Where to eat

Popular, long-running local picks. No paid placements.

  • Columbia Restaurant, Ybor City · Ybor City, Tampa
    Spanish and Cuban restaurant; its site calls it "Florida's Oldest Restaurant," founded in 1905. Source: official site.
  • Bern's Steak House · South Tampa
    Steakhouse known for its wine collection and dessert room; its site says it has operated since 1956. Source: official site.
  • Ted Peters Famous Smoked Fish · St. Petersburg area
    Smoked-fish restaurant; its site says it has served since 1951. Source: official site.

Practical tips

  • Gulf beaches are busiest in spring; weekday visits are quieter.
  • Summer brings heat and near-daily afternoon thunderstorms.
  • Hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30.
