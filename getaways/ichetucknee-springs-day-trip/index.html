Ichetucknee Springs: a quiet spring run near Fort White

Eight major springs feed the 6-mile Ichetucknee River in a North Central Florida state park best known for tubing and paddling.

By Claire Whitaker
AI-assisted journalist

Why go

Eight major springs feed the 6-mile Ichetucknee River in a North Central Florida state park best known for tubing and paddling. Reviewers have rated it well: see the review line above.

Itinerary

Day trip

  1. Morning

    Arrive at opening at the park's entrance on U.S. 27 in Fort White. The upper river is a National Natural Landmark, and the park recommends canoe or kayak in the cooler months.

  2. Midday

    Snorkel at Blue Hole, a spring the park says stays 72 degrees year-round, or picnic near the headspring.

  3. Afternoon

    Walk one of the park's three nature trails through forest and longleaf pine sandhill. In warm weather, tubers launch from Dampier's Landing at the South Entrance until 5 p.m.

Park, hours & fees

  • Ichetucknee Springs State Park (Florida State Parks)
    12087 S.W. U.S. Highway 27, Fort White. Open 8 a.m. until sunset, 365 days a year. $6 per vehicle. The Midpoint Tube Launch has been closed for repairs since March 17, 2026; tube rentals end at 4:30 p.m.

Hours, fees and address as listed on the official page when we checked on October 28, 2026.

Food

The park lists a general store, food service and rentals on its official page. Packing a picnic is an option; the park has picnic pavilions.

  • The Collective Food Yard and Garden (Fort White)
    A food-truck court in town, about a 10-minute drive from the park, with rotating local vendors.
    Yelp: 4.9 stars from 20 Yelp reviews (checked October 28, 2026; source listing).

Practical tips

  • Water at Blue Hole stays about 72 degrees year-round, according to the park.
  • Check the park page for tube-launch status before you go.
  • Hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30.
